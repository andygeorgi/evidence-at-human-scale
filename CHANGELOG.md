# Changelog

Releases are labeled `vMAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` and tagged in Git. Each entry lists what the release contains and its known limitations.

## 1.0.2, 24 November 2026

A corrective release. The contracts and the validation surface of the schema are unchanged, so everything that validated against 1.0.1 still validates.

### What changed

- **The commit check is time-consistent.** At commit, the reference gateway uses only what was available at the attempt. Evidence admitted after the attempt and a "not revoked" check made after it can no longer authorize it, and a time that is missing or unknown blocks. So does a time that cannot be compared with the attempt exactly (a fraction finer than a microsecond, or a zone offset that is not a real offset), and so does an accounting window without a readable start or end. A receipt whose own time cannot be read still counts against the limit. Only the capacity the ledger held by the attempt counts, so a reservation that starts later cannot change an earlier decision, while one that starts at the attempt does, and a cancelled or failed attempt keeps its capacity until its verification. A list that is missing (preconditions, evidence references, parameters) is never read as an empty one, and an event dated after the attempt gives no freshness. A what-if that changes a grant is evaluated as it stands (a version that is not yet issued is not "superseded" by an older one: a newer version must be in force), a per-action limit has a readable, ordered accounting window like a cumulative one (and is compared exactly, with the amounts shown as they were compared), a missing key is not the value `null` and `true` is not `1` (at any depth of a value), a freshness policy that states no kind blocks, an attempt is one entry in the ledger however many windows count it, and a replay that finds two receipts for one attempt refuses to guess which is its own. A grant or proposal that lacks a part the check reads (the limits, the scope, an amount in euro), a proposal whose amount is negative, not a number or stated twice, a freshness policy whose limit cannot be read, a state of the target, an approval time or a ledger that cannot be read, and a quantity that is not one finite, non-negative amount (it is compared exactly as written, with no rounding) block with a reason instead of ending in an error or passing, and every version of a receipt is held to the decision of its first. Business time, admission time, freshness, lifecycle state and the time of the revocation check are kept apart. Five reason codes were added (21 in all), each with a stored evaluation, and the commit-time tables say when each fires.
- **Every input is evaluated or rejected.** A contract id that is not a string, an unknown contract id, a file named like a contract that is not one, and a stage file without its `evaluations`, `cases` or `statements` collection each end in a diagnostic that names the file, the stage, the problem and what to do. An explicit empty collection is reported as such, a schema-only run no longer indexes the folder, and unrelated raw exports stay usable. JSON is read strictly: `NaN`, `Infinity`, a number too large for a float (a whole number as well as a fraction), a number with more digits than a double keeps, a repeated key and a document nested more than 40 levels are input errors, and `registry.json` is found in any letter case. A registry section of the wrong shape (a state of the target, a permission decision), a scenario or case row that cannot be judged (an unknown ledger mode, an empty list of mutations, an override path that is not a JSON pointer) and a row that is not an evaluation are input errors that name the file and the part, and `--json` reports a bad `--only` or `--skip` as a JSON result. A position in a list is read by its digits (a leading zero, a sign or a number too large to count is an error), a row that cannot be judged is reported also when its stage has no valid instance to run against, a folder that holds nothing to replay needs no registry to say so (NOT ASSESSED, not an input error), a registry that is missing is reported together with whatever else is wrong with the files of the stage, every diagnostic about the shape of a file that has the name of a stage file says how to free the name, a finding about a path that leads nowhere names the target, the operation, the path and what is missing there, and a part of a permission decision that is of the wrong kind is refused where the registry is read. Nothing disappears silently.
- **A response cannot define its own lifecycle.** Whether a response is `current` or `withdrawn` comes from its packet and its as-of time. A response about an invalidated packet that says it is current, omits its status, contradicts the packet's history or words withdrawn advice as current now fails. A withdrawn response carries the packet's own reason and nothing else (at most the introduction "This response was withdrawn:"), because words added to a reason cannot be checked. A free-form response must also state at least half of the packet's supported claims (a claim counts once, and only when a statement keeps one of its figures and one of its words, so a bare number does not count), and its status words, their negations ("did not fail") and its labels are checked in every kind of statement. A figure keeps its sign (also beside a currency code), any ISO 4217 currency counts, a record is named whole (`GR-552` is not `GR-5521`, proposals included), a statement that rests on an item with no text is held against no text, a response names its packet as a decision packet, advice that says it was never withdrawn or reinstates what was withdrawn fails, and a statement about a gap the reader may not read may not carry more than the permitted wording in its label or cite a part of the gap. The label of a statement is held to the rules of its text (certainty, closing what the packet keeps open, calling advice current or taking a withdrawal back: "not reinstated" denies it and is allowed), a certainty word is licensed only by the same word stated plainly in what is cited ("not independently verified" licenses nothing), a proposal says, each as a negation, that it is neither executed nor approved (a negation stops at "but", and "not only" is no negation), and its label may not say that it is, a sign in front of a currency counts (a dash with spaces does not), a sign after a colon counts, any currency sign of Unicode is a currency, and the reason for a withdrawal is built only from the records the packet itself names. These are mechanical checks, not a judgment of meaning.
- **What a reader may not know cannot change what they see.** The viewer's output is built from the inputs the permission decision allows, after the decision is bound to the requesting principal, tenant and purpose; a decision that names no tenant or no purpose binds to nobody. Whether a restricted record exists, how many there are, how fresh they are and what they concern make no difference to a reader who may not know of them, and a reader who may know only that coverage is limited receives exactly the configured wording, alone in its statement. An input the decision lets the reader read is not described as inaccessible, and a retrieval that says it was read may not cite a decision that does not let the reader read. This is snapshot validation and a reference projection, not an authorization service.
- **A claim is held to every reference.** A supported claim that cites a payload or several references is compared with all of them: it states the payload's status and a figure of it and no other, each figure beside the right field name (swapped quantities are found in either order, with filler words between, also when the field's own name is written out or the claim cites single values, figures are compared exactly as written, and a minus sign counts), and a pointer that does not resolve is reported. A claim that cites one value may not put a figure beside the name of another field of the record (also a field that holds text, and a name between quotation marks), whatever the figure is, and a figure after a colon is stated with its sign. A model inference may not state a figure its ancestry does not hold.
- **The executor holds the authority.** The assistant proposes, and the payment executor is the grantee of every grant and the executor of every receipt. A receipt under another principal's grant fails, as does an executor that is not registered and a receipt whose target is not the one of its proposal.
- **Verified no effect is scoped.** A rejection before submission, a verified no effect for one attempt, target and scope, and an unknown outcome are three different things, in the example, the figures and the guidance. A gateway rejection alone is not proof about every external effect, and a verified outcome must be verified for the receipt's own target and action by a check made at or after the attempt, from observations of that target that were made between the attempt and the check. What such a record says is free text and is not judged.
- **Teaching examples and hygiene.** The two AI summaries no longer state a figure or a comparison that their ancestry does not hold, and a generated "retrieved retrieved" was fixed. The hygiene scan now reads the scanner and its tests like any other file, and excuses a quoted sample only on its exact line. It finds a private term list at any path, and the list the environment names when it lies inside the folder (also through a link, after the file was deleted from the folder, as a hard link to it or to the list in the folder, or as a version in the history that has the list's content with either line ending), without ever reading or printing it; tries a private pattern written for a line on every line (also one that switches a flag off in a group); lets a denial excuse only the claim it negates ("not only" and a word about time excuse nothing, and the tail of a longer word is no denial); judges each file version at every path that held it; reads Git with replacement objects off and in NUL-separated fields; refuses a shallow clone; and runs over the whole history in continuous integration.
- **A calmer first read.** The README starts with the title, the problem, the architecture's response and one next step, with no version code or status block, and the banner follows the first paragraph so that a narrow screen meets the words first. The banner and the figures that led with codes now lead with the concept (the receipt states too), and the paper and guides name a thing before its identifier. The story table links to folders by name, and so do the links inside the first-use examples. The receipt figure no longer calls four outcomes the only receipt states and says that a verified no effect is recorded only after the target is checked, the late-correction figure queues the release under the approval and shows the approval revoked before the revised packet, the current packet says that only the carrier's proof of the second delivery is missing (the warehouse's own scan exists), a missing gap says that nothing was found in the sources that were read and not that nothing exists, and the template names an action before its identifier. Identifiers stay in the JSON, the schema, the diagnostic codes and the reference tables, and links to the sections of 1.0.1 that were renamed still land.

### Known limitations

- **Real-world effectiveness has not been evaluated.** The 12 tests, 70 fixtures and 12 matched baseline records are NOT ASSESSED, and the 47 domain parameters are UNSET. No test was run in this release.
- **The permission-bound output is a reference, not a service.** The repository checks stored outputs against stored decisions. It does not model counts, caches, relationships or view-time revocation, and it is not an authorization service.
- **The free-form response checks are mechanical.** They test named constraints and a minimum coverage of the packet's claims. They do not show that prose means what its packet means.
- **Accessibility has not been formally assessed.** The automated figure checks are not an accessibility assessment, and usability on a phone was not tested with people or devices. No conformance is claimed: NOT ASSESSED.
- **The limitations listed for 1.0.0 otherwise still apply:** the paper is Markdown only, the figures were checked in one browser, product statements are dated, and the example is one invented domain.

## 1.0.1, 24 November 2026

A corrective release. The contracts and the validation surface of the schema are unchanged, so everything that validated against 1.0.0 still validates.

### What changed

- **Validator correctness.** `validate_examples.py` reports five states: PASS, FAIL, SKIPPED, NOT ASSESSED and INPUT ERROR. A stage passes only if it evaluated something, so an empty examples folder, an empty example or a skipped stage is no longer a PASS. A malformed document produces a diagnostic with a fix instead of a traceback, and a stage that needs another stage says so. A run that leaves stages out ends with `PASS for the stages that ran` and says that its result says nothing about the skipped ones, and `check_all.py` does the same. A duplicated schema diagnostic was removed. The response validator now separates canonical validation, which compares a response with what its renderer produces and catches reversed meaning, from structural free-text validation, which checks named constraints and does not prove the prose is correct.
- **Corrected authorization and role semantics.** Permission is decided before anything about an input is disclosed, so a requestor who may not know that a restricted record exists cannot learn that it exists, is stale or was checked. Figure F09, example 04, the contract text and the guidance say so, and tests show that nothing leaks. The worked example keeps the requestor, the Decision Owner, the approver and the executor apart, and records that the controller holds two of the roles because the policy allows it. The packet is reviewed before the first approval, and the timeline says what was visible when.
- **Clearer onboarding.** The README starts from one invoice problem, puts the Quickstart before the architecture and offers a ten-minute path. The contract pages explain that the JSON examples are self-contained snapshots, why they repeat the metadata of the records they cite, and where the compact human view is.
- **Removal of internal and redundant material.** The evaluation catalogue no longer repeats constant, duplicated or process text. Its JSON files are the source; the CSV files and the test sections of the oracles page are generated from them, and each fixture lists the contracts it actually uses. Internal-process wording and obsolete repository attributes were removed.
- **Corrected evaluation claims.** The statement that no evaluation has been run was too broad. The contracts and synthetic examples are tested, and real-world effectiveness has not been evaluated. The evaluation tests, fixtures and baselines now use five defined states (RUN / PASS, RUN / FAIL, NOT RUN, NOT ASSESSED, UNSET). All twelve tests were reviewed: none can be run without an implementation, so all are NOT ASSESSED, where 1.0.0 listed them as NOT RUN. A row cannot claim a run without its command, input and output.
- **Prose and terminology.** Slogans, slash-heavy phrases and repeated qualifiers were rewritten, and several chapter titles now describe their subject instead of making a point. "Approver" replaces "accountable approver", the page that maps questions to chapters is now called the question index, and Figure F02 now shows that an approval and a grant are both recorded in C06 and what the gateway rechecks at commit.

### Known limitations

- **Real-world effectiveness has not been evaluated.** The 12 tests, 70 fixtures and 12 matched baseline records are NOT ASSESSED, and the 47 domain parameters are UNSET. No test was run in this release.
- **Accessibility has not been formally assessed.** The automated figure checks are not an accessibility assessment, and usability on a phone was not tested with people or devices. No screen-reader test, WCAG audit or PDF/UA check has been performed and no conformance is claimed: NOT ASSESSED.
- **The limitations listed for 1.0.0 otherwise still apply:** the paper is Markdown only, the figures were checked in one browser, product statements are dated, and the example is one invented domain.

## 1.0.0, 24 November 2026

The first public release of this repository, under the [MIT License](LICENSE).

### What it contains

- **The paper**: 15 Markdown chapters, from the overview to "Start with one decision, not a platform", with the glossary, [claims and limits](docs/claims-and-limits.md) and an [implementation guide](docs/implementation-guide.md). The [glossary](docs/glossary.md) gives one name to each concept and keeps legitimate variants apart, such as the three senses of "context".
- **The technical reference**: the [architecture reference](architecture/README.md) and 7 [contract pages](contracts/README.md), C01–C07, preserving all 41 field groups.
- **A schema bundle**: [`schemas/contracts-v1.schema.json`](schemas/contracts-v1.schema.json), a documentation-only JSON Schema (Draft 2020-12) with 38 valid and 64 invalid embedded examples. The [contract change record](contracts/CHANGES.md) pins its validation surface and says how a change to it is recorded.
- **A synthetic worked example**: 13 folders and 37 contract instances telling one invented supplier-payment story, with raw exports, a registry, stored gateway evaluations and an assistant response, and a validation command that is tested on Windows and Linux.
- **Evaluation guidance**: the [evaluation guide](evaluation/README.md), the oracles, human-outcome guidance and a catalogue of 12 tests, 12 matched baseline records, 70 fixtures and 47 domain parameters. Every status is NOT RUN or UNSET.
- **A figure set**: 11 figures and a banner, each with a caption, alt text and a text description. All are drawn SVGs, in light and dark, 360 px wide so that a phone shows them at their natural size; F02 and the banner also have a wide drawing for a large screen. F07 is drawn from a table, which is also given as text.
- **Tooling**: [`scripts/check_all.py`](scripts/check_all.py) and the checks behind it (examples, unit tests, link and anchor check, page-structure check, figure and hygiene checks), a hygiene scan that can also read the whole Git history and that lets the authority's name appear only on the exact lines that quote a public record's own title or address, an optional external-URL check, and a GitHub Actions workflow that runs them on Ubuntu and Windows.
- **A license**: the [MIT License](LICENSE) covers the whole repository: the paper and other text, figures, schemas, contracts, examples, scripts and tests.

### Known limitations

- **No evaluation has been run.** EV01–EV12, the 70 fixtures and the 12 matched baseline records are NOT RUN; the 47 domain parameters are UNSET. The repository's own checks are deterministic checks on invented data and say nothing about a real system.
- **The paper is Markdown only.** No PDF or Word edition is included in this release.
- **Accessibility has not been formally assessed.** Practical structural and visual checks were performed: every figure has alt text and a text description, every page has one H1 and no skipped heading level, and colors are always paired with a text label. No screen-reader test, WCAG audit or PDF/UA check has been performed and no conformance is claimed: NOT ASSESSED.
- **The figures were checked in one browser.** Each figure is drawn 360 px wide with no text below 13 px, and its text fits in Segoe UI, Arial and Verdana. That was measured in a desktop browser, in a phone-sized window and in a laptop-sized one, on the pages as GitHub renders them and with GitHub's own script for light and dark themes. It has not been tried on a physical phone or in a signed-in GitHub browser session. The switch to the wide drawing of F02 and the banner depends on the `media` attribute of a `<source>` element; where a page does not apply it, the narrow drawing is shown, and says the same. A reader whose GitHub theme differs from the color scheme of their system may see the wide drawing in the other scheme; it stays legible.
- **Product statements are dated.** Statements about Azure options carry their verification date, 17 November 2026, and may be out of date. Links to public records can move.
- **The example is one domain.** The supplier-payment story is invented to need no specialist knowledge. It shows the contracts at work; it is not evidence that the architecture works.
